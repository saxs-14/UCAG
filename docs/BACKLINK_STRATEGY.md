# Backlink Strategy

> Not code -- a plan. On-page SEO (this pass: sitemap, meta tags, canonical
> tags, schema markup, OG image) makes UCAG *indexable*. Backlinks are what
> make it *rank* once indexed -- Google weighs how many other credible
> sites link to a page, and it costs nothing but outreach effort, not ad
> spend. Prioritized by realistic effort-to-authority ratio for a new site
> with no existing link profile, not by which sounds most impressive.

## Why this matters right now

A brand-new domain with zero backlinks can be fully indexed (sitemap +
robots.txt fixed this pass) and still rank on page 5 for every query that
matters, because Google has no external signal that anyone else considers
the site worth citing. The fastest-moving, highest-value queries here
("matric results", "APS calculator", specific university admission
questions) are also the most competitive -- generic on-page SEO alone
won't out-rank established sites like universities' own admissions pages
or big bursary-listing sites without external validation.

## Tier 1 -- highest authority, do these first

**University admissions offices (.ac.za domains).** The single highest-
authority link type available in this niche, and there's a genuine value
proposition, not just a favor being asked: UCAG pre-qualifies applicants
against real, verified subject/APS requirements before they apply, which
means fewer confused or ineligible applications for the institution to
process. Email each Tier 1 institution's admissions/marketing office
directly, pitch it as "a free tool that helps your ideal applicants find
you and understand your requirements before applying," and ask for a link
from their prospective-students or FAQ page. Cold-email response rates
here will be low, but even 2-3 real .ac.za backlinks would meaningfully
outweigh dozens of low-authority ones.

**NSFAS and provincial Departments of Education.** Long-shot, high-value.
NSFAS's own site and provincial DBE career-guidance pages sometimes link
out to independent tools that help learners understand funding/admission
processes. Same pitch as above: this reduces confused/ineligible
applications and funding queries on their end.

**High school guidance counsellors.** Every South African high school has
a guidance counsellor or Life Orientation department pointing matrics
toward *something* for APS/application help every year. A one-page PDF
("share this with your Grade 12s") plus a direct email to guidance
departments at schools UCAG already has good coverage for, offering the
tool as a free resource, converts into both real backlinks (schools often
list resources on their own sites) and the kind of authentic word-of-
mouth traffic that itself earns organic mentions elsewhere.

## Tier 2 -- realistic, do these second

**Bursary/scholarship aggregator and career-guidance sites.** These sites
already link out heavily and often accept reciprocal or resource-page
listings (e.g. "other free tools for matrics"). Lower authority than
Tier 1 individually, but faster to land and useful in volume.

**EdTech/startup directories.** Product Hunt, BetaList, and South
African-specific startup directories (e.g. listings run by local tech
publications or accelerator programs) are low-effort, guaranteed
placements -- not high-authority individually, but a normal part of a new
site's link profile and easy to knock out in a single afternoon.

**Guest content on SA parenting/education blogs.** Pitch a genuinely
useful, non-promotional piece (e.g. "How APS actually works, explained"
or "The 5 most common matric application mistakes") to blogs that already
cover matric/parenting topics, with a natural link back to the calculator
as the practical next step. This is real work per placement but each one
is a contextual, editorial link -- the kind Google weighs most.

## Tier 3 -- ongoing, compounding

**"Matric results day" news cycle (mid-January every year).** This is the
single biggest annual traffic and linking opportunity for this niche in
South Africa -- national and local media all run matric-results coverage
and are actively looking for expert commentary and useful tools to
mention. Prepare a short data-driven angle in advance each year (e.g. "X%
of last year's Bachelor's passes didn't meet their first-choice
programme's APS -- here's what to do instead") and pitch it to education
journalists 2-3 weeks before results day, not on the day itself.

**Community mentions (Reddit r/SouthAfrica, matric-year Facebook/WhatsApp
groups).** Not direct backlinks, but genuine, non-spammy participation
(answering real questions, mentioning the tool only when it actually
helps) drives the kind of traffic and word-of-mouth that turns into
organic mentions and links elsewhere over time. Answering as a helpful
person who happens to have built something relevant works; posting links
unprompted reads as spam and can get the domain flagged.

**Keep it defensible.** Every link above is asked for because UCAG offers
genuine value to the site being asked to link, or from partners who use
it to solve a real problem. Never chase link farms, paid link schemes, or
irrelevant directory blasts -- Google's link-spam detection specifically
penalizes exactly that pattern, and it's the fastest way to undo the
on-page work from this pass.

## What this doc doesn't cover

Execution (who sends which email when) is deliberately not scheduled
here -- that's an ops/content-calendar decision for whoever runs outreach,
not a build artifact. Revisit this file if the actual outreach reveals
which tiers convert, and cut what doesn't work rather than defending the
plan as written.
